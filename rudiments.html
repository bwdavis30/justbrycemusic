Rudiments

As we move on, we should understand some of the rhythms and rudiments that are to be discovered in rudimental drumming. To extend, rudimental drumming is one of the instrumental groups with the widest variety of rudiemnts, serving as the band's backbone in rhythm.

Rolls

Before exploring some of the most common rudiuments included in rudimental drumming, it is important to first learn rolls. Rolls are some of the most difficult but fundamental aspects of drumming. Rolls are defined by the different types of strokes that are involved in the roll. The first are single strokes, which are not often seen in pitch-based drums that drag out their tones, such as timpani. Then, it continues on to double strokes, which is the most common roll with each stick landing 2 beats each in a RRLL format. There are also triple stokes and so forth, but these are not commonly seen and are instead replaced by buzz rolls. Buzz rolls are a roll in which involves pressuring the stick into the head to emulate a constant buzz sound for a specific amount of beats, but not defined to a certain amount of strokes.

Flams

Flams are a common rudiment seen in drumming where one beat serves as the actual downbeat and the other serves as a grace note. A grace note is a note that is slightly offset from its main beat, giving a sound somewhat resembling a gallop. Though, it is important to approach this with preciseness. The grace note is not to follow its main note too far behind since this can make you or the ensemble get off from the true beat. The grace note should only be very slightly off from the original. This can be approached by the downbeat starting higher than the grace note, and the grace note should be a tap with the stick raising ever-so-slightly. The grace note then can be altered by adding many different other rudiments on top of it, such as a buzz or double-stroking it.

Paradiddles

Paradiddles is another rudiment that is extremely common in writing. The name "paradiddle," according to many professionals, stems from the reading of the note. Para- represents the two alternating taps, while -diddle represents the two alike taps that resemble a diddle, which is short for a double stroke note. The sticking for this is RLRR when starting on the right and LRLL when starting on the right.

Hybrids

Now that you have learned a few of the important rudiments for drumming, you now must understand the importance of hybrid rudiments. If you think you have created a rudiment by clashing a bunch of different rhythms together, think again. Hybrid rudiments are rudiments that stem from one essential, basic rudiment that combines another or an unnatural sticking (or many other combinations for that matter). When someone creates a hybrid rudiment, it is often listed in a rudiment encyclopedia where drummers and composers can choose to include in their own performances. While some hybrid rudiments are widely unknown by many, others are extremely popular. For example, one of my personal favorites would be the book report rudiment, combining a flam, double stroke note, and paradiddle all in a single rudiment.

Whether it be common hybrids or simple rudiments, rudiments in general are detrimental to learn in drumming as they help improve your approach to technique and integration into music, which then can better your overall playing skills.
